## 1. THE GUIDING PHILOSOPHY — "EFFLORESCENT LEARNING"

Open with a philosophy section that defines the camp's signature pedagogy in the administrator's own words:

> **Efflorescent learning** = the intentional use of narrative; of mathematics living inside art and art living inside math; of historical problem-solving grounded in fantasy and design.

Frame "efflorescence" as the moment a hidden structure *blooms* into visible form (salt crystallizing on stone; a pattern surfacing from an equation). The teaching is built so understanding **blooms from a story** rather than being handed over as fact.

Express it through **four pillars**:
1. **Narrative as engine** — a god disables an invention; restoring it pulls every lesson forward.
2. **Math in art, art in math** — the rosette *is* a circle; the tessellation *is* a theorem. Never separated.
3. **Problem-solving in fantasy** — real historical engineering wrapped in adventure and design.
4. **Design as understanding** — making is the assessment; the artifact a child builds proves the concept they grasped.

**CRITICAL:** Weave the efflorescent lens into *every catalogue entry*, not just the intro. Each entry must contain a dedicated **"Efflorescent lens"** block: a 2–4 sentence narrative + design quest (which god strikes, what breaks, what the child rediscovers and makes) that turns that invention into a week of camp.

## 4. VISUAL DESIGN DIRECTION (make it striking)

Aim for an **illuminated-manuscript-meets-modern-museum-catalogue** aesthetic. This is the single most important instruction: it must look *stunning and editorial*, not like a generic web page.

- **Palette:** warm archival paper (`#F6F0E2`), deep near-black ink (`#14110B`), terracotta (`#C9512E`), antique gold (`#B8860B`), deep teal (`#1C7293`). Paper backgrounds for content; near-black for the cover, the efflorescent-lens blocks, and the weekly table.
- **Type:** a high-contrast display serif for headings (e.g., **Cormorant Garamond**), a readable body serif (e.g., **Spectral**), and a tight sans (e.g., **Archivo**) for labels/eyebrows. Use large italic accent words in headings (color them terracotta or gold).
- **Cover:** full-viewport, dark, with a faint museum/archival photographic background at low opacity + a radial vignette. Title set huge with one italic gold word. A "scroll to begin" cue.
- **Entries:** alternating left/right image-and-text layout; a large serif numeral (01, 02…) over each image; a civilization tag chip in the entry's accent color; the efflorescent-lens block as a dark card with a small ❧ ornament; the five facets as labeled spec rows with hairline dividers.
- **Imagery:** use **real photographs** (civilizations, artifacts, geometry, a child making art), NOT vector/SVG illustration. Apply a subtle paper-texture overlay site-wide at very low opacity.
- **Motifs to repeat:** thin inset border frames on images; short terracotta rule under each section title; generous whitespace; hairline dividers in spec rows.

**Avoid:** generic blue, centered body text, AI-slop accent underlines under titles, full-width colored bars, cramped spacing, text overflow. Run a visual QA pass and fix any collisions before finishing.
