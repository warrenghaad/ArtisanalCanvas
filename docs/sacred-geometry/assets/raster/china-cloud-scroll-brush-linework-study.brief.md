## What this image must communicate

The Chinese cloud scroll (云纹 yún wén) is a recurrent pattern in bronze, textile, ceramic, and lacquer traditions. Its distinguishing feature is that **the curl itself creates the visual surface**: compressed ink at the start of the curve, expanding and lifting toward the open end of the scroll, producing a three-dimensional sensation of volume and atmospheric movement entirely through line quality.

This image must make that linework logic visible as a study, not as a decorative reproduction.

## Drawing method to depict

- Ink brush or digital equivalent with brush pressure response
- Demonstrate the **qi stroke logic**: enter light, swell at mid-curve, lift at exit
- Show at least two cloud scroll units: one annotated with stroke direction and pressure labels, one unannotated (clean)
- The line's taper at entry and exit is essential — no blunt or rounded ends
- Ink density should vary: denser at inner curve compression, lighter at outer expansive arc

## Compositional structure

- Format: landscape, approximately 1200×800px minimum
- Left panel: single cloud scroll unit with stroke annotations (arrows showing direction, labels for "enter", "swell", "lift")
- Right panel: small field of 3–5 cloud scroll units in continuous interlocking rhythm

## Linework quality requirements (expressive-linework-raster criteria)

- Brush pressure variation is the core educational content — uniform digital strokes do not satisfy this brief
- The line must produce a sense of **atmospheric depth**: the compressed inner curve feels recessed, the open outer arc feels forward
- Visible brush texture or equivalent ink-spread effect is desirable
- No flat outline with fill — the line IS the form

## Cultural context notes

- Cloud scroll appears in bronze age through contemporary decorative arts — the pattern is not culturally restricted
- Do NOT depict dragons or specific ritual objects without context card pairing
- Reference appropriate: Shang/Zhou bronze vessel cloud-spiral patterns; Song lacquerware cloud borders; Han textile cloud pattern studies
- The scroll studied here is the spiral/comma form (S-curve scroll), not the layered cloudbank (阶层云)
